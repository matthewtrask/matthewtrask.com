Like a lot of software engineers, I have been using Claude Code extensively at my job and also for some personal projects.
At work, we are all in on Claude and while this isn't a blog post about Claude, I am impressed with what it can do. Especially
when you craft guardrails around it. 

One thing I have noticed is that the mental shift from writing a lot of code to reading a lot of code has been _hard_. I don't remember where,
but I saw the other day someone say how hard it is to defend code they own but didn't write. And it's so true.
Personally I've been too trusting of Claude, kind of yoloing it a bit. And this isn't very "software engineer-y" of me. However, 
how can you contend with what Claude can do? By that I mean what used to take me days or even weeks of time it can do in hours. A perfect example is this:

I saw a comment on Hacker News a few weeks ago. It was on a post announcing yet another indie web index. These have become a lot more common as people
are hunting for parts of the internet that aren't overrun by AI bots. A user left a comment saying something to the effect of "I wish
there was an index of the indexes". A brilliant idea I thought as I ran to Claude and started the conversation. I told it what I wanted, and had it spec out
the project. I gave the spec a generous once over and handed it off to Claude Code. Maybe 30 minutes later (I was watching the Red Sox game tbh), I got a working demo. 
**30 minutes**.
