Over the years, I've picked up a bunch of advice that spans software development. These are ones that I really like. 

### Make your bed.

In 2014, Admiral William McRaven, who commanded the Joint Special Operations Command for the U.S. Military gave the commencement speech
for the University of Texas. The speech was entitled "Make Your Bed", which alludes to an anecdote Adm. McRaven shares first after he does
a bit of reminiscing about his time graduating from the University of Texas before going off to SEAL Boot Camp. 
 
His first piece of advice "make your bed" is told like this: every day in SEAL training they were expected to make their bed.
It gives you a small win at the start of the day, and encourages you to keep going and doing more things. In software, we can view the bed as small tickets
that aren't as flashy or big impact but still ones that need to get done. Those small ones can pay dividends as they help 
clean up the code base, make it easier to work it, and could increase platform stability. Those small ones also give a developer a quick
win to start the day or the week before jumping into harder work. They now have a bit of confidence and energy to keep going. 

How can we ensure this happens? We should maintain a backlog of work that needs to get done but is low priority. Engineers should
be encouraged to reach into that work queue when they need a quick win and we should make sure the process allows them the time to do
the work to accomplish the task. And hey, even if the day goes sideways, they have a quick win to hold on to, just like a made bed is always
nicer to get into after a long day.
